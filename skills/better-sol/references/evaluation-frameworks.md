# Evaluation Frameworks

Use this reference when prioritizing ideas, scoring product opportunities, or making launch decisions with explicit criteria.

## Why scoring exists

Scores do not prove an idea is good. They make assumptions visible, comparable, and easier to revise when evidence arrives.

## ICE

ICE is useful for early-stage or hackathon decisions where speed matters and data is limited.

- Impact: how much the idea moves the target outcome.
- Confidence: how much evidence supports the estimate.
- Ease: how easy it is to execute.

Formula:

```text
ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease
```

Use ICE for quick triage.

## RICE

RICE is useful when user reach can be estimated.

- Reach: how many users/accounts/teams are affected in a period.
- Impact: how much the outcome improves for each reached user.
- Confidence: certainty in the estimates.
- Effort: person-time required.

Formula:

```text
RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort
```

Use RICE when comparing roadmap items with different audiences.

## Evidence ladder

Rank proof from weakest to strongest:

1. Founder intuition.
2. Competitor exists.
3. User interview quote.
4. Waitlist/signup.
5. User tries prototype.
6. User pays or commits.
7. Repeated usage.
8. Revenue or retained users.

## Validation metrics

Choose one primary metric per sprint:

- interviews completed
- qualified signups
- wallet connections
- successful transactions
- retained users
- grant/judge acceptance
- paid conversions

## Anti-bias rules

- Lower confidence when evidence is only internal opinion.
- Penalize ideas where Solana is decorative.
- Penalize ideas where distribution is unknown.
- Separate hackathon demo quality from startup quality.
- Rescore after new evidence rather than defending the first score.
